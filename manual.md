Fabrivoy — Quick Guide
Fabrivoy keeps notes on every supplier you visit — at a trade fair, a sourcing trip or a factory tour. Everything is saved on your phone and works with no internet. Nothing is uploaded.

The four tabs
Tab	What it is
Suppliers	Your list of visits. Search by company, booth number or product name.
Samples	A pickup checklist for samples you were promised.
Export	Turn your notes into a Word or Excel file.
Settings	Edit your question lists, photo saving, and delete all data.
1. Add a supplier
On Suppliers, tap + New.
Photograph the sign at the booth or entrance. Fabrivoy reads it and fills in the company name and booth number, but only fields you haven't already filled. Always check what it filled in. It reads Latin letters only, not Chinese characters. Tap Read details from sign photo to run it again.
Fill in or fix Booth number, Company name, Hall / area, Office location and Factory city. Booth number is optional, so leave it blank for factory visits.
Every photo is saved with the supplier even if reading the text fails.

2. Business card and contacts
Photograph the card under Business card. Fabrivoy fills in a contact (name, phone, email, website, WeChat, WhatsApp). Use Read contact from card to run it again.
Under Contacts, use + Add contact for more people. Tick WeChat, WhatsApp or Email when you have actually connected with them, so you know whom you still need to follow up.
3. Products
Tap + Add product for each item you look at:

Photos, Product name, Description, Main features
MOQ (minimum order)
Price by quantity: tap + Add price row and enter a quantity and price for each tier
Sample taken and a Sample note
Product questions (see below)
4. Questions
Two checklists you can edit under Settings:

General questions are asked once per supplier (factory or trading company, where the factory is, markets, certificates and audits…).
Product questions are asked for each product (certification, customisation, best price, MOQ, whether you can pick up a sample on the last day…).
Some questions have answer buttons: tap an answer, and tap it again to clear it. In Settings you can add, reorder and delete questions, and give any question answer choices (comma-separated, e.g. "Yes, No"). Leave the choices empty if you want to type the answer.

5. Samples tab
Any product with a sample answer on its product questions and Sample taken unticked appears under To pick up. Tap Mark taken when you've collected it. Tap a row to jump to that supplier.

6. Notes and other photos
Use Notes for impressions and follow-ups, and Other photos for anything else (price lists, the stand, the factory floor). Tap Done at the bottom when you finish a supplier. Changes are saved automatically.

7. Export
On the Export tab:

Export as Word doc (photos inline) — one page per supplier with each product's photo next to its details. This is the best choice for reading and sharing. It opens the iPhone share sheet: email it, save it to Files, send it by WeChat, and so on.
Spreadsheet + photos (.zip) and Spreadsheet only — an Excel table with one row per supplier, for sorting and comparing. The zip includes your photos in folders per supplier; extract the whole zip so the photo links work. If the file is too big to send on a weak connection, use "spreadsheet only".
Export before you delete anything. Your photos on the phone are never reduced; only the exported copies are made smaller.

Settings
Save to iPhone Photos app: also copies every new photo into your normal Photos library. It is off by default.
Delete all suppliers removes all suppliers and photos, after two confirmations. Your questions and settings are kept.
Tips
Take the sign photo first, before you walk in, so every photo has the right supplier.
Take it straight-on and keep the text in focus. Close-ups read better than wide shots.
Do the Word export at the end of each day, and send it to yourself as a backup.
Everything works in Airplane Mode.
If you delete the app, all its data is deleted with it, so export first.
Problems?
If the text from a photo is wrong, fix it by hand. Send photos that were read incorrectly, with a note, to the person who gave you the app. They help improve it.
